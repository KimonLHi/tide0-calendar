How the sand surface score is computed

The sand-surface estimate is a 0–100 composite of six components:

  • 40% tide exposure: predicted water level and the recent curve; lower levels and more time below about +1.5 ft MLLW raise this component.
  • 20% recently wetted/retreating sand: recent high water and how long ago it occurred, plus the retreat from that high.
  • 22% waves: moderate wave-energy fit (height² × period) is favored; very high recent energy applies a recovery/disruption penalty.
  • 10% tide phase: a smoother falling-tide phase is favored over a fast-rising tide.
  • 5% surface preservation: wind, humidity, sunlight and elapsed time since high water provide a heuristic drying/preservation adjustment.
  • 3% observed water-level residual: recent NOAA observed level minus its tide prediction, blended toward neutral as it gets stale or forecast time grows.

If wave energy or the observed residual is unavailable, that component is omitted and its weight is redistributed proportionally across the available components. The overall favorability score then combines 78% sand surface, 14% estimated crowding, and 8% time convenience. Weather does affect the surface-preservation component; air temperature and rain are displayed as context only. These are heuristic weights, not beach-width measurements.

How the score is normalized

The hourly chart compares each raw model score with all hourly scores pooled across the full trailing-year reference, so it answers how this hour compares with the yearly average rather than only similar seasons or clock hours. The calendar uses a separate daily-best percentile, comparing each day's best available raw-score hour with all historical daily maxima in that same trailing year, which accounts for choosing the best hour from each day. Neither score is a probability of a good run. The top-in-N label is a base-10/logarithmic view of the upper tail, capped at the number of reference observations; with about 8,760 hours, this data supports at most about 1-in-8,760 empirical resolution, not claims like 1-in-100,000. Reference window: 2025-11-01 through 2026-10-31 (8,760 modeled hours pooled across all local times and seasons). The reference uses NOAA hourly tide predictions and ERA5-Ocean hourly waves; weather and observed-water residual are neutral in the historical series. It advances monthly. If matched history is unavailable, the raw weighted score remains the fallback.
